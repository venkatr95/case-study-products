# Design principles

**Date:** 22 October 2026

These principles decide arguments about the SkillBridge interface. They are not a brand system for other products.

## 1. Start from what the person already claims

The first sentence of a comparison counts skills they listed. Extras stay visible and are not failures. An empty list is an invitation to add skills, not a report card.

## 2. Say the limit in the same voice as the product

"This compares what you entered with a role profile. It does not predict hiring, pay, or interviews." The line is on the welcome card, in the banner, and in Settings. It is not a disclaimer behind a tooltip.

## 3. Prefer a sentence to a score

Matched, partial, and not in your list have definitions on the page. Counts are words. A percentage is out of the system even if a chart would look more "data-like."

## 4. Small plan, editable plan

The generated set stops at three. Every action has an outcome and a first step the person can rewrite, replace, or delete. The product does not assign homework.

## 5. Show the source

A role profile has a source sentence and a date. Illustrative profiles say they are not O*NET and not an employer posting. A custom profile says the person wrote it.

## 6. Confirmation before anything sticky

Resume suggestions are unchecked. The save button waits for a check. Sample data does not replace real work without a dialog. Delete asks. Corrupt storage is not overwritten.

## 7. Calm visual weight

Paper, a serif headline, and one teal action. Slate for "not in your list." Amber for partial. Red only for destructive confirmation. No confetti, no rank, no flame for a gap.

## 8. The words do the access work

Color repeats the label. Focus is visible. Targets are at least 44px. The comparison is a list, so a screen reader gets the same sentence as the eye.

## Trade-off we accepted

People who want a match rate will not find one. That is a deliberate miss for the guardrail. Usability testing has to watch whether the miss becomes a dead end. The fix, if needed, is a clearer explanation, not a quiet percentage.
