# Proto-personas

**Status:** Hypotheses. Not derived from interviews.
**Date:** 22 October 2026

These sketches exist so the interface has a concrete reader. They must be replaced or discarded after the research plan runs. They are not target-market segments with sizes. No market size is stated anywhere in this case study, because none was measured.

## Hypothesis P1 — Career changer in daily operations

**Sketch:** An adult who runs a shift, a floor, or a service desk and has started reading postings with titles like coordinator, operations associate, or customer success.

**Situation we are designing for:** They can point to scheduling, customer conversations, and spreadsheets. They cannot tell which of those a new title expects, and a 40-item course list does not tell them what to do on Thursday.

**Need the design serves:** A role profile in plain language, a place to say "I do this, and I am not sure," and three actions that use a skill they almost have.

**Risk of this sketch:** The real first step may be a conversation or a posting, not a skill inventory. If interviews say that, P1 should not drive the home screen.

**Illustrative data, not this person:** Maya Chen in the prototype is a fictional shift lead used to demonstrate the comparison. Maya is not P1. Do not cite Maya as evidence.

## Hypothesis P2 — Early-career coordinator

**Sketch:** Someone in the first years of office work who already writes updates and tracks tasks, and who is asking whether that is "enough" for a business analyst or project coordinator title.

**Situation we are designing for:** They have language from their job, little confidence it will count, and a habit of looking for a score that says they are ready.

**Need the design serves:** Confidence as its own question, a comparison that does not become a grade, and permission to edit a profile that is not an official standard.

**Risk of this sketch:** They may need proof they can show an employer. That need is out of scope. The product refuses to be that proof. Research should check whether the refusal makes the product useless to them.

## Who is not a persona here

Recruiters and employers screening candidates are non-users in the PRD. The interface has no seat for them: no candidate rank, no shared shortlist, no "top applicant" state.

## How findings should change these sketches

- If the jobs people name are outside customer, operations, and coordination work, change the role family before polishing the five profiles.
- If people describe themselves with different level words than Exploring, Practicing, Comfortable, and Strong, change the skill form.
- If both sketches care more about a specific posting than about a role family, the comparison object should become a posting the person pastes, with the same no-score rule. That is a strategy change, not a color change.
