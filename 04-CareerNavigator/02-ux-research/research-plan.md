# Research plan

**Status:** Planned. No participants have been recruited. No interviews have been run.
**Date:** 22 October 2026

The PRD asks for 5–8 interviews before treating the problem as confirmed, a review of 3–5 alternatives, and a prototype test with 5 people. The competitive review is done and cited. The interviews and the usability test are not.

## Research questions

1. When someone last considered a different role, what did they do in the first hour, and what did they put down?
2. How do they decide which of their current tasks "count" for a role they have not held?
3. Where do long lists of skills or courses cause them to stop?
4. What would they need to see before they trusted a skill comparison that is not from an employer?
5. How do they talk about confidence in a skill they use at work but have never been trained in?

These questions are about recent behavior. They do not ask people to evaluate SkillBridge.

Round 1 comes before any change to the role family. Round 3 can use the prototype that exists now. Findings from round 3 do not replace round 1.

## Participants

**Include:** Adults who, in the past six months, looked at a role different from their current or most recent one, or who are in the first three years of a career and have compared themselves with a job posting. English-language sessions for this plan.

**Exclude:** Recruiters or hiring managers whose main job is screening candidates. People under 18. Anyone who would be a coworker or a reporting line of the interviewer.

**Sample-size rationale:** Six interviews are enough to hear whether the same workaround shows up more than once. They are not enough to estimate a market. Five usability sessions are enough to find repeated breakdowns in a single flow. They are not enough to claim the PRD's 80% and 70% targets. Those targets need a later, larger round.

## Logistics

- Sessions recorded only with consent, stored locally, deleted after notes are checked by the participant if they ask.
- No payment is defined yet. Do not recruit until a compensation method is real.
- Tooling: a video call plus the local prototype for round 3. No account is required.
- The prototype stores data in the participant's browser. The moderator resets or deletes that data at the end of the session from Settings, and asks the participant to close the tab.

## Consent and privacy

Read the consent points aloud and keep a copy:

- The session studies the design, not the person.
- They can skip a question or stop.
- Notes will not include phone numbers, addresses, dates of birth, health, or other sensitive details they happen to mention. If a resume example contains those, the note records "removed" instead of the value.
- Quotes, if used later, need a second yes. This folder contains no quotes.
- SkillBridge does not need a resume. If they paste one during round 3, the paste is not written to disk by the product. The moderator does not copy it into the findings file.

## Analysis

After each interview, write a note with: what they did last time, the artifact they used (posting, spreadsheet, course list, conversation), a pain in their words, and a design implication labeled as interpretation.

Across notes:

- Separate **evidence** (what they said they did), **interpretation** (what we think it means), and **design implication** (what would change in SkillBridge).
- Do not average opinions into a persona and then call the persona a finding.
- Update `research-findings.md` only with material from notes. Leave the template rows empty until then.
- If three or more people describe a different first step than "compare skills," revisit the core flow before more visual design.

## What this plan must not do

- Invent participants, quotes, or percentages.
- Ask "would you use an app that…" as the main question.
- Treat Maya Chen's sample path as evidence about users.
