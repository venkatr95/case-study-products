# Visual direction — Rewear

**Name of the direction:** Morning paper.

A wardrobe app can look like a nightclub or like a recycling poster. Rewear looks like a well-lit table: warm paper, ink, and a terracotta action color. The grid is the visual event. Type does the branding. Fraunces for the wordmark and titles, Figtree for everything you operate.

## Why this and not the neighbors

- Whering and Acloset’s public positioning is social and stylistic. A high-gloss fashion UI would imply that Rewear is another one of those.
- A leaf-green “eco” palette would moralize, which the PRD forbids even in color.
- A pure utilitarian gray would hide the grid. The paper background lets garment illustrations and photos sit in a frame without a card shadow contest.

## What you should notice in five seconds

A two-column, then four-column, grid of garments with names. One terracotta button. Almost no chrome.

## Illustration

Items without photos use a flat garment silhouette in the item’s color, on a cream square. White and beige get a hemline stroke so they do not vanish. Multi is split navy and beige. These are not generated fashion photos and not people.

## Desktop

From 960px the prototype becomes a left-hand navigation and a wider grid, still the same screens. It is a review surface for the mobile hierarchy, not a separate information architecture.

## What success looks like in critique

Someone can tell the active tab, the primary button, and the item’s name without asking. They cannot tell the app’s opinion of their body, because it has none.
