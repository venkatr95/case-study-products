# Proto-personas — Rewear

**These are hypotheses for design discussion. They are not research-backed personas.** No names below are participants. No quote is real. Replace this file after the interview round rather than “adding color” to it.

Two personas are enough for the MVP. A third “fashion enthusiast” was considered and rejected so the product would not drift toward a social stylist.

---

## Maya — weekday decision, hypothesis

**Snapshot:** 31, hybrid job, a wardrobe that already covers work and weekends. Mornings are short. She often repeats the outfit that is easiest to see.

**Context we are assuming**

- Dress code is “smart, not a suit,” with occasional evenings.
- She owns more than she rotates.
- She has taken mirror photos and rarely looks at them again.
- She is not trying to build a public style identity.

**Goals**

- Leave on time in something that fits the day.
- Remember the navy knit that is in a different drawer.
- Avoid buying a “work top” that is almost the same as two she owns.

**Frictions, hypothesized**

- The closet shows the last outfit, not the options.
- She will not spend a Sunday photographing everything.
- A suggestion that cannot explain itself feels like being dressed by a stranger.

**What Rewear asks of her**

- Add a few real items, or learn the idea on the sample wardrobe.
- Set occasion and weather preference when she builds an outfit.
- Mark an outfit worn if she wants recently-worn to mean anything. If she does not, the product still stores the outfit.

**Success for Maya:** she can save a work outfit in one sitting and find it the next morning.

**Do not design for:** a score of her body, a lecture about fast fashion, a feed of strangers.

## Sam — short-trip packer, hypothesis

**Snapshot:** 28, packs for work trips of about three or four nights, a few times a year. Packs late. Brings extras “just in case,” then wears half of them.

**Context we are assuming**

- He already knows a few outfits that work in a city he is visiting.
- He does not want to re-decide those outfits in the hotel.
- He will not maintain a closet app every day. He might maintain it the night before a trip.

**Goals**

- Choose the outfits first.
- See the actual list of garments, with the shared shoes listed once.
- Check items off while they go in the bag.

**Frictions, hypothesized**

- Outfit ideas and the packing list live in different notes.
- Duplicates are how bags get heavy.
- A weather API that demands location feels like too much for a packing list.

**What Rewear asks of him**

- Save the outfits before the trip exists.
- Enter nights as a reminder, not as a calendar integration.
- Type a destination only if he wants a label. It is not geocoded.

**Success for Sam:** two or three outfits become one checklist he can pack against.

**Do not design for:** a social trip board, a shopping list of missing “capsule” pieces, a claim that he packed lighter than last time. We cannot know that.

---

## How to use these without fooling ourselves

- Critique a screen with “would Maya know what to do next?” as a heuristic, then throw the answer out if a real session disagrees.
- Do not put Maya or Sam on a portfolio slide as “our users.” The portfolio slide says proto-personas, unvalidated.
- Recruitment should be able to find people *like* these situations (recurring dress decision, recent short trip). It should not look for these biographies.
