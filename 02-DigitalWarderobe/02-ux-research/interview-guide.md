# Interview guide — Rewear

**Use:** 45-minute discovery interview. Do not mention Rewear, outfits-from-tags, or the prototype until the optional last section, and skip that section if time is short.

**Moderator note:** Ask about the last time, not about a typical time. If they generalize, ask “when was that?” Silence is fine. Do not finish their sentence with a feature.

## Intro (3 minutes)

Thanks for talking. I’m learning how people decide what to wear and what to pack. There is no product to react to today. I’ll ask about recent days, not about your style.

I will take notes. Recording only if you already agreed. You can skip any question or stop. I won’t ask you to show photos of yourself.

## Getting dressed (15 minutes)

1. Think about the last morning, or evening, when getting dressed took longer than you wanted. When was that?
2. Walk me through it from when you started until you left. What did you pick up, put back, or forget?
3. Where were the clothes you considered? On a chair, in the wash, in another room, in a photo?
4. What almost made it, and why did you reject it?
5. Had you worn any of those items in the previous few days? Did that matter?
6. Did weather, a dress code, or a plan change the decision? How did you know?
7. How did you feel when you finally decided? Annoyed, fine, late, something else?

## Remembering what you own (8 minutes)

8. Is there a piece you like and rarely wear because you forget it? What gets in the way?
9. How do you keep track today, if you do? Photos, a list, another app, nothing?
10. If you have tried an app or a system, where did you stop using it? What were you doing when you stopped?
11. What would you not want to photograph, or not want stored? You don’t have to explain your body. “I don’t want pictures stored” is a complete answer.

## Packing (10 minutes, if they have a recent trip)

12. Think of the last trip where you packed clothes. How many nights, roughly, and what kinds of plans did you have?
13. Did you decide outfits before you packed, or pack a pile and decide later?
14. What did you pack and not wear? What did you wish you had?
15. Did any item do more than one job? How did you notice?

If they have not traveled, ask instead: the last time you needed clothes for two different plans in one day. How did you choose?

## Attributes, without leading the list (5 minutes)

16. When you decide two things go together, what are you looking at? Let them answer before any prompt.
17. Only if they are stuck, you may offer this as a checklist, not as the right answer: color, how warm it is, how dressed-up it is, what you wore recently, the plan for the day. Ask which of those they actually used in the story they told.

## Close (4 minutes)

18. If you could change one thing about that last morning, or that packing hour, what would it be?
19. Anything I should have asked and didn’t?

Thank them. Do not pitch.

## Optional, only after the stories (skip by default)

If the team has already decided to show a paper sketch in the same session, limit it to one question: “What would you expect to happen next?” Do not ask if they like it. Do not demo suggestions. Preference in an interview is not task success.

## Notes the moderator should capture

- Date and city only if they volunteered it. No address.
- Their words for the annoying moment.
- Workarounds.
- Attributes they named before any prompt.
- Whether packing was outfit-first or item-first.
- Anything they refused to store.

Do not write a persona in the margin during the session.
