# Competitive analysis — Rewear

**Reviewed:** 22 October 2026.
**Method:** Official store listings and vendor pages where they could be opened, plus one competitor’s comparison page clearly labeled as such. Prices change by country and date. User counts below are publisher claims, not audited figures. This review did not install the apps or test them with a screen reader, so accessibility comparisons are limited to what the public pages say.

## Whering

**Source:** Google Play listing “Whering: Your Digital Wardrobe,” Whering Ltd, updated 5 October 2026, accessed 22 October 2026. <https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.whering.app&hl=en_GB>

**What the listing says**

- Positions itself as a free social styling and wardrobe app. The listing claims “9 M+ users.” Play shows a 5m+ download bracket. Those are different measures. Neither was independently checked.
- Add clothes from a database it describes as over 100 million items, from retailer sites, from a Chrome extension, or from the person’s photos with background removal.
- “Dress Me” shuffles the wardrobe. Outfit Maker is behind a Style Pass. The Play About text reviewed here does not print the price of that pass.
- Packing lists, cost per wear, wear rate, color palette, wishlists, moodboards, and friends’ wardrobes are all described.
- What’s new, on that listing: the planner suggests outfits from the wardrobe and the weather, and trips can be planned by changing location.
- Data safety section, developer-provided: the app may collect location, personal info, and other types; it may share photos and videos with third parties; data is encrypted in transit; the user can request deletion.

**Implication for Rewear:** Packing, cost per wear, and outfit suggestions are not unmet features. Whering already describes them, and it ties planning to weather and location. Rewear’s difference is the refusal of the social graph and of location, and the requirement that a suggestion explain its tags. Do not market Rewear as the first app with a packing list.

## Stylebook

**Source:** Stylebook FAQ, accessed 22 October 2026. <https://www.stylebookapp.com/faq.html>

**What the FAQ says**

- One-time charge of $4.99 in the US, not a subscription.
- Five ways to add clothes without taking photos, including a catalog, web clipping, and imports. AI background removal is also offered.
- Outfit Shuffle creates outfit ideas. The FAQ points to a 2016 explanation of that feature. This review did not re-test how Shuffle chooses pieces.
- iPhone and iPad, with iCloud sync. The FAQ says it does not run on Android or desktop, and that as of 2025 the company was not planning Android.
- Closet data is described as stored on device and in iCloud if enabled. The FAQ says Stylebook cannot see the closet and does not sell customer information. That is their statement, not an audit.
- Pack-by-outfit is described in the FAQ’s “is it worth it” answer. Stylebook’s features page, indexed with a 14 March 2025 date and reviewed through search results on 22 October 2026, describes adding an outfit to a packing list so its clothes are added, plus a checklist and cost per wear from price and logged wears. Treat the features-page detail as **to re-verify on the live page** if this case study is updated.

**Implication:** Stylebook is the closest philosophy: your clothes, your combinations, packing from outfits, a real manual path. It is iOS-only and paid up front. Rewear does not need to copy the free-form collage canvas. It does need the manual path to feel finished, because Stylebook already proved people will tag clothes themselves.

## Acloset

**Sources**

- Apple App Store listing text for “Acloset - AI Fashion Assistant,” English text indexed 20 October 2026. <https://apps.apple.com/cl/app/acloset-ai-fashion-assistant/id1542311809>
- Acloset’s own widget note, 14 October 2026. <https://www.acloset.app/announcements/6-41-1-1791974915033/>
- Indyx’s comparison page, accessed 22 October 2026. This is a competitor writing about Acloset. <https://www.myindyx.com/versus/acloset-vs-indyx>

**What those sources support**

- Acloset’s listing says the free tier includes its features up to 100 items, with a subscription for more space. It describes an AI stylist, personal-color and silhouette analysis, daily outfits from weather and schedule, a calendar, cost per wear, and a community it claims has 4 million users.
- The 14 October 2026 note describes home-screen widgets that show weather-based outfit recommendations, including a next-day preview after 6 p.m.
- Indyx states that plans start at $3.99 a month and run up to $24.99 depending on closet size, that the free tier is ad-supported, and that Acloset has a social feed and a secondhand marketplace where payment and shipping are arranged outside the app. **Those price, ad, and marketplace details are a competitor’s account.** They were not confirmed on an Acloset price screen in this review.

**Implication:** Acloset shows the demand for “what do I wear today?” and also the risk Rewear is choosing not to take: body and color analysis, a feed of other people’s clothes, and a shop. Rewear’s suggestion screen has to feel more trustworthy than a black-box daily pick, because that is the thing it is giving up Acloset’s automation to protect.

## Indyx

**Source:** Indyx, “Acloset vs. Indyx,” accessed 22 October 2026. <https://www.myindyx.com/versus/acloset-vs-indyx>

**What Indyx says about itself**

- Free catalogue with no item cap stated on that page, background removal, and adding by receipt email or URL.
- Unlimited outfits, packing lists, and cost per wear when outfits are put on a calendar.
- Insider membership for analytics, a customizable display, and receiving outfits from other people. The membership price is **not** printed in the section reviewed. Third-party roundups have quoted $12.99 a month or $74.99 a year. Treat that price as **unverified** here.
- Human-styled lookbooks from $150, using the person’s real wardrobe.
- iOS and Android.
- Resale inside Indyx is described as a later vision on that page, not as a live checkout.

**Implication:** “Outfits from what you own” is also Indyx’s mission, delivered partly by human stylists. Rewear should not pretend to be a stylist service. Its bet is that an explained rule is enough for the MVP test, and that a person can always build the outfit by hand.

## Cladwell

**Sources, all accessed 22 October 2026**

- <https://cladwell.com/pricing>
- <https://cladwell.com/app>
- <https://cladwell.com/>
- <https://cladwell.com/faq>

**What Cladwell says**

- Free plan: build and organize a closet and track what you wear. No card required.
- Your Stylist, on the web price they publish: $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year (USD) after a 7-day trial for new users. They note that Apple or Google billing can differ.
- Clothes can be added from a catalog they describe as 15,000 items, from a product link, or from a photo. Their pages say most people never take a photo. That is their claim about their users.
- Daily outfits that consider weather, color analysis from a daylight photo, and “see it on me” are part of the paid stylist, not the free closet.
- Platforms named: iPhone, iPad, Android.
- They say AI features can have usage limits, and that they do not use photos to train Cladwell or third-party models. The training statement is theirs.

**Implication:** “You do not have to photograph everything” is already a marketed path. Rewear’s manual form is still worth doing, because it keeps color, season, and dressiness in the person’s words instead of matching them to a catalog illustration. Do not claim this onboarding idea is unoccupied.

## Opportunity that is still specific

The crowded part of the market is “digitize your closet and let something else dress you,” often with a social or commercial next step.

The opening Rewear is designed for:

- A private closet. No follower graph and no public outfit.
- A suggestion that can be read. Occasion, season, dressiness, color, and recent wears are visible reasons. The person can dismiss or save.
- Photo as an attachment, never as a scan, and never as a body reading.
- Weather as a choice, so the product does not need location. That is a real trade against Whering’s and Acloset’s live weather. It is the right trade for the PRD’s privacy constraint.
- Packing as the union of chosen outfits, with duplicates collapsed. Several competitors already pack from outfits. Rewear’s standard is that the collapse is obvious in the UI (“In 2 outfits”) rather than a hidden dedupe.
- Manual entry with the same dignity as a photo, including when the photo fails.

## What not to claim in a portfolio review

- That competitors lack search, filters, cost per wear, or packing. Several describe those.
- That Rewear’s suggestions are “smarter.” They are narrower and legible.
- Any market size, download total, or conversion number that is not in a cited primary source.
- That a screen-reader pass was done on Whering, Stylebook, Acloset, Indyx, or Cladwell. It was not.
