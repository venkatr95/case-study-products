# UVP and positioning — Rewear

## Positioning line

**Create more outfits from clothes you already own.**

Supporting line used in the product, not as a slogan on every screen:

Catalogue what you have, then build an outfit for an occasion or a trip. Photos are optional. Rewear does not comment on your body or tell you to buy more.

## Who it is not for

- People looking for a community or a stylist-for-hire. Whering, Indyx, and Acloset already occupy parts of that.
- People who want the app to shop for them.
- People who want a verdict on their coloring or silhouette.

Saying no to those jobs keeps the wardrobe from turning into a store.

## Difference, in product terms

| Choice | What the person gets | What we give up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rules you can read | A “why this was suggested” list tied to tags they set | The surprise of a generative stylist, and any claim of taste |
| Manual entry as a peer of photos | A wardrobe even when the camera fails or they decline photos | A magic “point and the closet fills itself” demo |
| Weather preference, not a forecast | No location permission | Less “it already knows today’s rain” |
| Packing from saved outfits | One line per item, with the outfits named | A free-form suitcase that includes things that are not in an outfit. The person can still add those items to an outfit first. |
| Cost per wear only with price and wears | No fake precision | No number on a new, unworn piece |
| Sample wardrobe of 12 labeled items | The value moment before setup | A risk that reviewers confuse sample clothes with theirs. The banner and the item detail both say they are illustrative. |

## Category language

Call it a **digital wardrobe and outfit planner**. Do not call it a stylist, a sustainability tracker, or a closet social network.

UI nouns, used the same way in the prototype and the specs:

- Item, not “SKU” or “piece” (the PRD says item)
- Outfit
- Occasion
- Weather preference
- Trip
- Packing list
- Dressiness, for the casual / smart / formal tag. “Dressiness” was chosen over “formality” in the form label because it is plainer. The case study still uses formality when talking about the data field.

## Tone

Practical, specific, and a little warm. Name the clothes. Say what will happen when something is deleted. Do not say “good choice,” “flattering,” “guilt-free,” or “save the planet.”

## Message map

| Moment | Message |
| --- | --- |
| First screen | Create more outfits from clothes you already own. |
| Empty wardrobe | Add an item you already wear, or look through the sample wardrobe. |
| Photo failure | The photo could not be saved. You can try again, or save the item without a photo. |
| Suggestion | Why this was suggested, as a list of tags and gaps. |
| Packing | Shared pieces are listed once. |
| Settings | In this prototype, data stays in this browser. Nothing is uploaded. |

## Brand voice check

If a sentence could sit in a clothing ad, rewrite it until it could sit in a well-made utility. The visual direction can be warmer than a settings screen. The words stay plain.
