# Research plan

**Status:** Planned. No participants have been recruited and no sessions have been run.

## Purpose

Learn whether the cash-timing problem is real for the hypothesized segment, which recent behaviors people already use, and whether a 30-day estimate is the right thing to test next. The plan is intentionally about past behavior. It does not pitch MoneyMind in the interview.

## Research questions

1. When people last decided whether they could spend, what did they look at, and what happened afterward?
2. How do they track bills that fall between paydays today?
3. Where have they been wrong about money that “looked available”?
4. What would they need to see before they trusted a forward-looking number?
5. How many items are they willing to enter before a tool feels useful?
6. Do irregular-income and regular-payday situations fail in the same way?

## Method

- Semi-structured interviews, 45 minutes, remote or in person.
- One recent episode per participant (“the last time you weren’t sure you could afford something”), then a short tour of whatever tool they actually opened, if they are willing.
- No concept test in the same session. Concept testing is a separate usability study so the interview does not teach the solution.
- Optional diary: three spending decisions over two weeks, only if the interview shows people cannot recall a recent episode. Not started.

## Participants

**Target:** 5–8 interviews before any scope change, matching the PRD discovery note.

**Why that number:** Enough to hear the same job twice and to notice a contradiction. Not a sample for percentages. The 70% comprehension target belongs to the later usability test, not to these interviews.

**Include, as hypotheses:**

- At least three people paid on a regular cycle who also have a monthly bill larger than one paycheck.
- At least two people with irregular income.
- A mix of people who have tried a budget app and people who use a bank app or a note.

**Exclude:** Professional traders, advisers, bookkeepers working for clients, and anyone under 18. Do not recruit only people who already love a budgeting method. They will describe that method.

Screener: `screener.md`.

## Logistics

- One moderator, one note-taker when possible. If alone, record only with consent and say so.
- Session length 45 minutes plus 10 minutes of buffer.
- Incentive: decide before recruiting and state it in the invite. No amount is promised here.
- Prototype is not required for this round.

## Consent and privacy

- Say the session is for a portfolio concept, not a bank.
- Do not ask for account numbers, passwords, card numbers, or exact balances if the person is uncomfortable. Ranges are enough.
- Do not record screens that show full account numbers. Ask them to hide balances if the recording would capture a real bank app and they prefer that.
- Store notes without names. Keep a private key of participant IDs separately if recontact is needed, and delete it when the study is closed.
- Do not paste real financial figures from sessions into this public case-study folder.

## Analysis

1. Write notes as observations, separate from interpretations.
2. Code incidents: trigger, tool used, what the balance hid, outcome, emotion only if they stated it.
3. Cluster jobs. Do not force them into the two proto-personas.
4. Mark any quote with the participant ID and the question that prompted it.
5. List design changes that would follow if a cluster is real, and what would be dropped.

The findings document is a template until this analysis exists. Do not backfill it with imagined quotes.

## After the interviews

Update `research-findings.md`, then revisit the MVP only where evidence contradicts an assumption in `01-product-analysis/assumptions-and-risks.md`. Run usability testing on the prototype only after the problem still looks like cash timing.
