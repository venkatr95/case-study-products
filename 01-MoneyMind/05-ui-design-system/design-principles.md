# Design principles

## 1. Show the dip before the reassurance

The lowest point is a peer of the big number. A calm total that hides the date of the shortfall fails the job, even if the total is arithmetically right.

## 2. The person can recompute it

Every figure on the explanation maps to a starting balance, an entered item, or a set-aside. If a row cannot be tied to something they typed, it does not belong.

## 3. Uncertainty is a label, not a mood

“Estimate” is always visible. The interface does not add fake precision, confidence percentages, or a shaded range that implies a model.

## 4. No shame, no cheerleading

Shortfalls are described with dates and amounts. There is no scolding and no celebration when the number is positive. A positive safe-to-spend is not a prize.

## 5. Nothing is saved by accident

Scenarios, previews, and state previews say they are not part of the plan. Delete asks. The destructive action is not the focused button.

## 6. One pool, said plainly

The product does not look like a multi-account bank. Currency, set-aside, and “on this device” keep the boundary obvious.

## 7. The ledger beats the chart

The chart is a glance. The list and the table are the record. Color is never the only cue for the lowest day.

## 8. Prototype honesty

If a production capability is missing, the screen says so. Background notifications and encrypted storage are examples. A polished illusion would be a trust bug.
