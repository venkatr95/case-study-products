# Interview guide

45 minutes. Remote. Neutral. Ask about the last two weeks, not about an ideal kitchen.

**Status:** Unused. No sessions have been run. Do not fill quotes from this guide.

## Before you start

Read the consent lines aloud. Confirm recording. Tell them there are no right answers and they can skip anything.

Do not say “food waste,” “expiry,” or “FreshLoop” until they use a word first. If you need a topic label, say “food you already have at home.”

Do not show the prototype in the first 30 minutes.

## Opening (5 minutes)

1. What does a normal weekday dinner look like in your household this month?
2. Who decides what to cook, and who shops?

## Recent behavior (20 minutes)

Stay on a specific night or shop. If they generalize, ask “What about the last time?”

3. Think of the last time you opened the fridge or a cupboard and found something you had forgotten. What was it, and what did you do?
4. Think of the last time you bought something and later noticed you already had it. Where were you when you decided to buy it? What list, if any, did you check?
5. Walk me through the last time you chose what to cook on a weeknight. What did you look at first: the food, a recipe, a person, or something else?
6. What did you do with ingredients that were left over from that meal?
7. Have you ever kept a list of food at home? If yes, what was on it, and when did you stop updating it? If no, what have you tried instead (notes, photos, memory, a store app)?

Follow-ups, only if they raise the topic:

- What words were printed on the package, if you noticed any?
- Did anyone else add to or ignore that list?

## Decision rules they already use (10 minutes)

8. When you are not sure how much of something you have, what do you put on the shopping list?
9. What do you do with a package whose printed date has passed? What do you wish you knew? (Listen. Do not correct them. Do not teach food safety in the interview.)
10. Are there foods you avoid for a diet or an allergy? How do you check a new recipe today? What would you not trust an app to decide?

## Household (5 minutes)

11. If two people cook, how do you avoid buying the same thing twice? Where does that break?
12. What would have to be true for you to still be updating a food list seven days from now?

## Close (5 minutes)

13. What should a tool never say about the food in your kitchen?
14. Anything about the last two weeks of cooking or shopping that I did not ask?

Optional, only if time remains and they ask what this is for: one sentence — “A concept that starts from food you already have, then suggests a meal and a short shopping gap. It does not judge whether food is safe.” Then stop. Do not demo unless they ask to see it. If you demo, use the sample kitchen and say the items are fictional.

## Moderator rules

- One question at a time.
- Do not ask “Would you use an app that…”
- Do not complete their sentence with Use soon, best-before, or staples.
- Write their words. Mark your own hunch in a separate column.
- If they describe throwing food away, do not praise or scold.
