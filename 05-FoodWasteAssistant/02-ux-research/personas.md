# Proto-personas

**These are hypotheses, not research.** They compress the PRD segments so design discussions have a name. They contain no quotes, no ages presented as facts, and no usage statistics. Replace them when `research-findings.md` has evidence.

## Hypothesis persona A — Household planner

**Segment:** Primary, from the PRD. Someone who plans meals and shops for more than themselves.

**Situation we are designing for:** Several dinners a week. Food sits in a fridge, a freezer, and a cupboard. Another person may eat or shop without updating any list.

**Job hypothesis:** When it is time to decide dinner, they want to see which of their own ingredients need attention and a practical dish that uses them, so the next shop is shorter and less duplicated.

**Behaviors we are assuming, not observing:**

- They already look at a recipe app or a note before they look at the back of the fridge.
- They will type a name if the rest of the form can wait.
- They get uneasy if an app tells them food is safe or must be thrown out.

**Design consequences if this persona is real:**

- Pantry home leads with a count of Use soon, not a recipe feed.
- Shopping lines show the reason an amount was left off or kept.
- One device is a known gap, called out in settings, not a hidden limit.

**What would retire this persona:** Interviews where the person who shops does not cook, and the person who cooks will not log food. The job would split across two people, and a single-player pantry would be the wrong product.

## Hypothesis persona B — Small-pantry cook

**Segment:** Secondary, from the PRD. A student or budget-conscious cook with less storage and a sharper cost when something is forgotten.

**Situation we are designing for:** A short list. One or two locations. Less patience for a household setup.

**Job hypothesis:** When money and space are tight, they want to cook from what is already paid for before buying more.

**Behaviors we are assuming:**

- Duplicate buys are memorable because the item was expensive relative to the week.
- A long onboarding or an account wall ends the session.
- Dietary filters matter for some of them, and a false “free from” claim would be worse than no filter.

**Design consequences if this persona is real:**

- Welcome offers “Start my kitchen” with no account, and a labeled sample kitchen for the demo.
- Quick add is a name only. Quantity and date stay optional.
- Diet copy says the filter is a list of ingredients on the sample recipe, not an allergen judgment.

**What would retire this persona:** If secondary recruits describe the same shared-household conflicts as persona A, do not keep a separate product path. One flow can serve both until research splits them.

## Who we are not designing the MVP for

- A household that needs simultaneous edits. That is an open PRD question, not a screen.
- Someone seeking medical nutrition advice. Non-goal.
- Someone who wants the app to decide safety from a date or a photo. The product refuses that job.

## How to use these in a critique

Say “if persona A is right” before defending a screen. Do not say “users want.” The case study uses the same rule.
