# Design principles

FreshLoop should feel like a kitchen notebook that refuses to bluff. The principles below are the rules used for the prototype. They are not the result of a brand study.

## 1. Inventory before inspiration

The first number on the home screen is how many items the person dated inside their window. Recipes come after that count. A feed of dishes would copy the products the PRD is trying not to be.

## 2. Say the arithmetic

Have, Short, Check, Missing, Staple, and Optional are visible. A covered ingredient is listed as left off, not deleted from the explanation. A unit the app cannot compare stays on the shopping list.

## 3. The person owns the date

Best-before, use-by, opened-on, or none. Opened-on is never promoted into an expiry. The interface does not say expired, unsafe, or throw it away.

## 4. Optional is still a choice

Salt, pepper, and water can be assumed, and the switch is in settings. Olive oil is not a staple. Optional recipe ingredients start off the list. Diet filters hide optional flagged ingredients too, and they say they are not an allergen judgment.

## 5. A missing provider is a state, not a decoration

Barcode lookup fills a form. Receipt scan says it is not connected. Reminders say they stop when the page closes. Sample recipes say they are original. The sample kitchen says it is fictional.

## 6. One correction is always nearby

Undo follows a pantry change. Delete asks. Unreadable storage is downloaded before it is erased. A duplicate can be merged or kept.

## 7. Color supports a word

Citrus and clay mark attention. The word Use soon or Date passed is what a screen reader and a grayscale print both get. Shape and text do the classification. Color does not do it alone.

## What these principles rule out

- A waste streak or a score.
- A green “safe” badge.
- A one-tap “cook this” that edits the pantry with no proposal.
- Silent household sync.
- Copying FoodKeeper storage times into the matcher.
