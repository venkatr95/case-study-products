# Test scenarios

Five tasks. The set is the PRD’s “at least 5.” Task 1 and 2 are the metric tasks. Tasks 3–5 protect the guardrails.

Use a fresh profile. Speak the scenario. Do not point at buttons.

## Moderator script

“I am going to ask you to use a prototype called FreshLoop. I did not write a test of you. If something is confusing, that is useful. Please think aloud. I will stay quiet until the task is done. Some food in the sample kitchen is made up.”

After each task: “What did you just change?” and “Did the product tell you anything about whether the food is safe?”

## T1. Add an item and find a recipe

**Start:** Empty kitchen, Welcome.

**Scenario:** “You bought spinach yesterday. Add it to the kitchen with whatever details you would bother to type. Then find a recipe that uses it.”

**Success:** The spinach row shows the name they typed and any details they typed. They open a recipe whose ingredient list includes spinach. They do not need a date for success. If they enter a date, they can say it is a reminder.

**Notes:** This is the 75% task. “Relevant” is satisfied here by a recipe that lists the item they added. Do not grade a different recipe as failure if they explain why they opened it.

## T2. Shop the gap, including a Check

**Start:** Sample kitchen. Moderator sets it up and says the items are fictional. Open Yogurt oat bowl, then the shopping review.

**Scenario:** “You want to make this. Put on the shopping list only what you still need. Leave off what you are sure you have. Tell me about the yogurt.”

**Success:** They can point to at least one ingredient left off because the pantry covers it, and they can say yogurt was not subtracted because the units do not match, or they leave yogurt on the list for that reason. Editing the amount is allowed.

**Notes:** This is the 70% task. Failure if they believe the app converted a piece of yogurt into grams.

## T3. Dates

**Start:** Sample kitchen, Use soon.

**Scenario:** “Which of these should you pay attention to before you shop, and what is different about the ginger and the yogurt?”

**Success:** They separate a passed best-before from an opened-on date, and they do not say the app told them either food is unsafe. Wording can be their own.

## T4. Cook and undo

**Start:** Sample kitchen, Spinach and chickpea skillet.

**Scenario:** “You made this for two. Update the kitchen. Then put the spinach back the way it was.”

**Success:** They confirm the update and use Undo. The spinach amount returns. Hint allowed only after they say they are stuck.

## T5. Reminders and a filter

**Start:** Settings, reminders off.

**Scenario:** “Turn reminders on if you want them. If you do not want them, refuse them. Then show only recipes that fit a vegan filter, and tell me what that filter guarantees.”

**Success:** Refusal does not trap them. If they allow, they can say the reminder is only while the page is open, after reading it. On Cook, they notice lemon chicken is gone, and they do not describe the filter as an allergen certification. The disclaimer can be read aloud by them. Do not ask them to recite it.

## Example of a bad prompt

“Click Use soon, then open the skillet, and confirm the Check state on yogurt.” That prompt teaches the answer. Do not use it.
