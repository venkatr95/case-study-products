# Test scenarios

Five tasks. The facilitator reads the situation and the goal, then stays quiet. Success is observed, not declared by the participant's confidence alone.

## Moderator script

"This is a draft of a route comparison. The streets and numbers are examples, not instructions for a real walk in Seattle. I will ask you to do a few things. I will not tell you if you are right while you work. Please talk about what you are looking at. You can stop anytime."

## Task 1 — Set a preference

**Situation:** You want a walk with no stairs, curb ramps where they are known, and no steep blocks over about 8%.
**Do:** Set that up and save it.
**Success:** Step-free is selected, or custom settings match those three values, and the participant saves or explicitly uses them for the trip.
**Not success:** They only open the screen. They turn stairs off because the preset name sounded medical.

## Task 2 — Choose a route

**Situation:** Start at Cal Anderson Park, south path. Go to Pike Place Market, First Avenue entrance. Choose the route that fits the settings you just saved.
**Do:** Compare and choose.
**Success:** They choose Madison gentle, and they can say it matched on confirmed data. Choosing it after saying Pike and Seneca "is probably fine" is not success.
**Error:** Choosing Pine Street direct. Choosing Pike and Seneca while calling it a match.

## Task 3 — Explain the evidence

**Situation:** Stay on the comparison or open a route.
**Do:** In your own words, what is the difference between confirmed, community-reported, and unknown on this screen?
**Success:** Rubric score 2, from the test plan.
**Prompt if silent for a long pause:** "What would you not assume?" Then mark the task assisted.

## Task 4 — A trip that does not match

**Situation:** Keep the same settings. Look at a trip from Cal Anderson Park to Volunteer Park's upper loop. The facilitator may use the prototype control "Show a trip with no confirmed match," and should say they are using a review control.
**Do:** Say whether any route fits, and what you would do next.
**Success:** They say it does not match, mention the hill or the unknown stairs, and do not describe the route as acceptable without changing settings.

## Task 5 — Report status

**Situation:** You found stairs on the Pine Street link.
**Do:** Report it. Then say what status it has, and whether other people can treat it as a checked fact.
**Success:** They submit, read pending review, and say it is not verified. If they open Your reports, they can tell their row from the sample verified report.
**Error:** They describe the report as already confirmed or fixed.

## Optional, if time

- Delete the saved preferences and say what changed.
- Decline location and say whether the app used their position.
- Increase text size and reread a status.

## Closing questions

1. What, if anything, sounded like a promise?
2. What would you still check outside this app?
3. Was there a word you would change?

## Success for the session overall

The concept is worth a second iteration if most participants complete tasks 1 and 2 unassisted and score at least 1 on task 3, and if nobody leaves believing the product guaranteed the route. That is a planning rule for the author, not a result.
