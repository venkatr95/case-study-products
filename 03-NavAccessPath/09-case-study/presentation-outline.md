# Presentation outline

Eight slides. About eight minutes. Say "hypothesis" whenever a user need is not from a study.

## 1. Title

AccessPath. Routes for how you move.
Role, proposed timeline, status: prototype, validation not run.
Visual: wordmark on the paper background, or `assets/screenshots/home.png`.

## 2. The failure

A short walk can include stairs or a hill. A step-free toggle often answers with one line and a gap that looks like silence.
Visual: none, or the Pine Street card inside `results.png`.
Say: this is the problem hypothesis, not a measured rate.

## 3. What already exists

AccessMap personalizes grade and stairs. Google and Citymapper ship step-free or wheelchair options. Wheelmap already paints unknown in grey, for places.
Visual: a simple table from the competitive analysis. Do not show competitor logos you have not licensed.
Say: the gap is comparison with evidence, not "an accessible map."

## 4. The rule

A match requires confirmed data for every setting the person turned on. Unknown blocks a match. A community all-clear does not confirm. Nothing is called fully accessible.
Visual: three chips.
Say: this is the product decision. It is testable.

## 5. The sample trip

Cal Anderson to Pike Place. Madison matches and takes longer. Pike and Seneca is unknown. Pine conflicts and is shortest.
Visual: `assets/screenshots/results.png`.
Caption: preference fit is the default. Shortest is still available.

## 6. Detail and disagreement

Segments are text. The south arcade keeps two pending reports and does not merge them. A new report lands as pending review.
Visual: `place.png` and `status.png`.
Say: trust is a status, not a tone of voice.

## 7. What I left out

No turn-by-turn. No account. No live feed. No basemap. No guarantee.
Visual: the offline or outside-pilot screenshot.
Say: empty states draw no route.

## 8. What is still unknown

No interviews, no usability sessions, no data license. Next: five interviews, then five concept-test tasks that are already written.
Visual: none.
Close on the question the test must answer: do people treat "unknown" as a warning, or as permission?
