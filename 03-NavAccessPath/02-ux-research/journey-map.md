# Current-state journey map

**Status:** Hypothetical current state, before AccessPath. Built from the problem statement and the competitive review, not from interviews. Every pain is a hypothesis.

**Traveler in this sketch:** Someone planning an unfamiliar walk from Capitol Hill toward Pike Place Market, wanting to avoid stairs. The neighborhood is a scenario, not a studied trip.

| Stage | What they might do | Touchpoints that exist | Pain hypothesis | Opportunity for AccessPath |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decide to go | Pick a time and a destination. | Calendar, a message thread. | The access question is easy to postpone. | Preferences saved on the device so the question is already answered. |
| Ask for a route | Open a general map. The first walk is the shortest. | Google Maps or Apple Maps. | The shortest walk can include the Pine Street stairs. **Hypothesis.** | Keep that route, and label the conflict. |
| Look for an accessible mode | Hunt for a wheelchair or step-free control. | Google wheelchair option, Citymapper Step-free, AccessMap where it covers the city. | The control is one switch. Grade and unknown blocks are hard to see. **To validate in each app.** | Presets plus per-route evidence. |
| Check the destination | Search the market entrance. | Place page, Wheelmap pin, a phone call. | A green or grey pin does not describe the walk. Grey at least means unknown. | Entrance attributes use the same three evidence words as the route. |
| Resolve a disagreement | One note says there is a step. Another says there is a ramp. | Crowd map, photos. | The person must invent a tie-break. | Show both notes and do not merge them. |
| Leave | Follow the line. | Turn-by-turn in the general map. | A barrier shows up on the sidewalk. | This MVP stops at the choice. It does not pretend to guide the last meter. |
| After | Tell a friend, or post nothing. | Message, a map report. | Unclear whether a report changed anything. | Pending status, timestamp, and withdraw. |

## Emotional arc — hypothesis, not measured

Uncertain while comparing sources, falsely reassured by a single accessible toggle, then frustrated if a stair appears. AccessPath's intended arc is narrower: cautious at the start, informed at the choice, and unsurprised by gaps because the gaps were labeled.

## Moments that matter in the prototype

1. Saving a preset.
2. Seeing three different statuses on one trip.
3. Opening the unknown route and reading which fields are blank.
4. Submitting a report and reading "pending review," not "verified."
