# Jobs to be done

The primary job is the PRD's. The others are hypotheses about situations, not validated segments.

## Primary job

When I am planning a trip, I want to compare routes against my access requirements, so I can choose a route with fewer unexpected barriers.

**Access requirements in the MVP:** avoid stairs, prefer curb ramps where known, and an optional maximum grade.

**Done looks like:** A route is chosen, its status matches the settings, and any unknown answer to those settings was visible before the choice.

**Not done:** The app promises the route is barrier-free. The app starts turn-by-turn guidance. The app files a verified city work order.

## Related jobs — hypotheses

| Situation | Job | MVP response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The short route is tempting. | Help me see the cost of the short route in access terms, not only in minutes. | Pine Street direct stays visible with confirmed stairs and a 10% grade. |
| I am not sure the data exists. | Help me tell a surveyed fact from a gap. | Unknown chip. Unknown blocks a match when that setting is on. |
| Someone else reported a problem. | Help me know whether anyone has checked it. | "Community report, pending review" versus "Verified." |
| Two reports disagree. | Help me see both, not a blended score. | South arcade lists both, with a line that they are not merged. |
| Today is a stroller day. | Help me start from different settings without building a new profile. | Stroller or luggage preset, still editable. |
| I was wrong, or the barrier is gone. | Help me take a report back. | Withdraw, while the status is pending. |
| The app does not know this place. | Help me not mistake silence for a route. | Outside-pilot and no-data screens. No line is drawn. |

## Forces — hypothesis

- **Push:** A past trip that failed at a stair or a hill.
- **Pull:** A comparison that shows its evidence.
- **Habit:** The general map is already open and is good enough when the person knows the neighborhood.
- **Anxiety:** A new app could be confidently wrong. The copy is written to lower that risk by refusing a guarantee, which may also make the product feel less decisive.

## Job the product refuses

"Tell me this way is safe." AccessPath can say what it confirmed against the settings the person chose. It cannot say the trip is safe, complete, or officially accessible.
