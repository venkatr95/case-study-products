# Screener

**Status:** Draft for a future study. Do not treat answers as collected.

Use a short form. Reject gently when someone is outside the study. Do not ask for a diagnosis.

## Questions

1. In the last three months, have you planned a walking trip, or a transit trip that included a walk, in a place you did not already know well?
   - Yes / No. **No:** end.
2. On that trip, did stairs, steep hills, curb ramps, or step-free entrances matter for you or for someone you were with?
   - Yes / No. **No:** end, unless question 3 is yes.
3. Were you planning for yourself, for someone else, or both?
4. Which of these were true that day? Select any.
   - I wanted to avoid stairs.
   - I wanted to avoid a steep hill.
   - I was with a stroller, cart, or luggage.
   - I was using a wheelchair, walker, cane, or other mobility aid.
   - I was planning for another person who had one of these constraints.
   - None of these.
5. Which tools did you use to plan? Select any. This is for mix, not for a quota that excludes map users.
   - A general maps app.
   - A transit app.
   - A site or app specifically about wheelchair access.
   - Calling the place, or asking a person.
   - Street-level photos.
   - I did not look anything up.
6. Do you work for a mapping, transit, or accessibility-rating company?
   - Yes / No. **Yes:** end for this round.
7. Are you willing to talk for 45 minutes about that trip, without sharing medical records?
   - Yes / No.
8. Preferred format: video, phone, or in person. Any access need for the session itself (captioning, a screen reader, a slower pace).

## Quotas for the first round

- 5 to 8 completed interviews.
- At least 4 "yes" on avoiding stairs or steep hills for themselves.
- At least 2 companions, stroller, or luggage trips.
- At least 2 who use a mobility aid, if the screener reaches them without pressure. If it does not, do not keep the form open longer just to fill a disability quota. Note the gap.
- At least 2 familiar with the candidate pilot city. If Seattle is dropped, change this line.

## What not to do

- Do not pay extra based on a diagnosis.
- Do not require a wheelchair user to represent every access need.
- Do not recruit only from people who already contribute to Wheelmap or similar, for this first round. A later round can include expert mappers.
