# Design principles

1. **Evidence before reassurance.** Say what is confirmed, what was reported, and what is unknown before offering a choice. Do not soften a gap into "likely fine."
2. **Settings, not a diagnosis.** Presets describe stairs, curbs, and grade. They do not name a body or a disability.
3. **The list is the product.** A drawing can help. It cannot be the only place a fact exists.
4. **A match is narrow.** It means the selected checks are confirmed and inside the limits. It is not a promise about the whole street.
5. **Status is a word.** Color, shape, and line pattern repeat the word. They do not replace it.
6. **Reports stay humble.** Pending review is a different fact from verified. The interface does not thank someone into thinking the city has checked their note.
7. **Quiet chrome.** The trip is the loudest thing on the screen. The visual system is paper, ink, and a deep green used for the action, not for "go, this is safe."

## What the visual system refuses

- A green badge that means "accessible."
- Confetti, streaks, or a mascot.
- Medical iconography, wheelchairs as decoration, or inspiration language.
- Red and green as the only difference between conflict and match.

## How the principles show up

The primary button is green because it is the action color, and the match chip uses a check and the sentence "Matches your preferences on confirmed data." The green is not the message. The conflict state uses a triangle, a double border, and the word Conflicts, on a warm barrier tint, so it is not only "the red one."
