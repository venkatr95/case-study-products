# UVP and positioning

## One line

Find routes that work for your mobility needs, not just the shortest route.

## Category

AccessPath is a route comparison for access conditions. It is not a general map, a place-rating community, or a transit agency app. Those are inputs it would eventually cite. In the prototype they are sample sources with dates.

## Positioning statement — hypothesis

For people comparing a walking trip against stairs, curb ramps, and steepness, AccessPath is the comparison that shows whether each route matches those settings on confirmed data, where the data is only a community report, and where it is unknown. Unlike a single step-free toggle, it does not hide the route that fails, and it does not treat a gap as an all-clear.

## Reasons to believe in the concept

These are design decisions, not measured outcomes.

- The match label is narrow: "Matches your preferences on confirmed data."
- The following sentence says a match is not a guarantee the path is barrier-free.
- Unknown grade or an unknown curb ramp blocks a match when that setting is on.
- A community report that a block has no stairs does not count as confirmation.
- Conflicting entrance reports are listed with timestamps and are not merged.
- Distance and time stay on the card so a longer match can be chosen with open eyes.

## Reasons a person might not switch — hypothesis

- Their city already has a step-free router they trust.
- They need live transit, which this MVP does not integrate.
- They want a guarantee. AccessPath will not give one.
- The evidence language may feel slow if the only question was "which way has no stairs?"

## Tone

Specific, calm, and literal. The product does not congratulate, diagnose, or apologize for a person's body. It names stairs, grades, curb ramps, sources, and dates.

## Name

AccessPath. The wordmark sits with a two-path mark: one solid, one dashed. The mark is the product idea, a route you can confirm beside a route you cannot.

## Message hierarchy

1. Compare by your access settings.
2. Read confirmed, community-reported, and unknown as different facts.
3. Choose with the gaps showing. Nothing here is a guarantee.
